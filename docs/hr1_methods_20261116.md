# The methods of family 1 (HR1): the bench, and beats against the ECG on public neonates (2026-11-16)

Plan: HR1LAB (memory `project_hr1lab_plan_20261115`, approved by Alex on 2026-11-16), steps F1 (bench),
F2 (beat-level validation on a public neonatal set) and F3 (our own captures against the ward
monitor's heart rate, and the MS100). Scripts: `pulsenest/hr1_bench.py`,
`experiments/hr1_board_mirror_20261116.py`, `experiments/hr1_mimic_perform_20261116.py`,
`experiments/hr1_methods_20261116.py`. Checks: `test_pulsenest/hr1_bench_test.py`. Offline only: no
board, no new capture, nothing in the library changed, nothing adopted.

Method labels (`docs/nomenclature.md`): `HR1_THR` = today's HR1 detector (threshold crossing),
`HR1_TERMA` = two event-related moving averages (Elgendi 2013), `HR1_SSF` = qppg's slope sum function
(Zong 2003), `HR1_MSPTD` = multi-scale peak and trough detection (Bishop & Ercole 2018). Publication
rules: `R0` today's (mean of 5 RR, `hr1_sqi` = 1 − CV / 0.15), `R1` own after Nonin (reject an RR more
than 20 % from the median of the last 5 accepted, publish their median), `R2` own (accept an RR only
within 15 % of `HR3_SAC`'s period). A chain is a detector and a rule.

## Why

Family 1 is the only family that times each beat. Our captures have no ECG waveform (Alex,
2026-11-16: plan as if there will be none), so the beat-level question cannot be answered on our
instrument. The MIMIC PERform neonates (Charlton et al. 2022, Physiol Meas 43 085007) have the
bedside monitor's PPG and ECG together, in the open (ODbL): the beat-level evidence comes from there,
with the caveat that it is another instrument.

What the makers do (prior-art index §2.1): Nonin averages 4 or 8 beats and flags the beat that does
not match the current pulse interval (the model of `R1`); Nellcor matches crests and valleys and
tests each pulse's derivative; Masimo recognises pulses by curvature. None publishes its detector.
Today's `HR1_THR` is a simplified Pan-Tompkins.

## Methods

### The bench (F1)

- **One front end for every method** (Alex, 2026-11-16; library rationale §5.5): HR3's 4th-order
  Butterworth low-pass at 15 Hz at the AFE rate, negated, decimated to 100 Hz, 2nd-order Butterworth
  high-pass at 0.5 Hz. Today's moving average stays only to reproduce the board.
- **Detectors, papers' values**, causal and fed in blocks (the study feeds a whole run, HR1LAB will
  feed 20 ms blocks; the checks show both give the same beats):

  ```
  HR1_THR    rising crossing of 0.6 x running max (decay tau 1.5 s), refractory 0.185 s   (library)
  HR1_TERMA  clip < 0, square; MA 111 ms vs MA 667 ms + 0.02 x mean; blocks >= 111 ms; max in block
  HR1_SSF    slope sum over 0.17 s; learning 8 s; eye-closing 0.34 s; onset from the 2/3 point
  HR1_MSPTD  6 s windows, 20 % overlap; local-maximum scalogram; lambda = scale with most maxima
  ```

  Causal adaptations, said before measuring: TERMA's moving averages run twice forward with the peak
  average delayed to the beat average's centre (the published code runs them forward and backward),
  and its offset uses the running mean since the start (published: the whole record); qppg rescales
  with the learning period's range (published: the record's) and replays the learning period as the
  original does; MSPTD processes a window when it is complete (latency up to 6 s).
- **The bench reproduces the board.** The library's chain in the bench (moving average, `HR1_THR`
  without interpolation, `R0`) against the HR1 the board published, every 0.5 s on the 19 stored
  sessions: same validity in 97.9–100 % of the rows, HR1 within 1 % in 99.1–100 %, `hr1_sqi` within
  0.05 in 99.1–100 % (`experiments/hr1_board_mirror_20261116.py`). The checks also compare the bench
  with a literal per-sample port of `_hr1_update`, quirk included: a crossing inside the refractory
  is not a beat but restarts the interval, so the next RR is measured from it.

### The public neonates (F2)

- **Data.** MIMIC PERform Testing Dataset, the 100 neonates: 10 min each of the monitor's PPG, ECG
  (lead II) and impedance respiration at 125 Hz, from the MIMIC-III Waveform Database (Zenodo 6950488,
  CSV, MD5 checked, downloaded on 2026-11-16 with Alex's go, kept outside any repository). The 100
  training neonates are kept for choosing parameters out of sample and were not opened for this part.
- **The PPG into the bench.** The pleth is normalised to 0–1 and rises with the pulse (upstroke
  steeper than downstroke in 99 of 100 records), so it is negated to look like raw OT and upsampled
  ×4 to 500 Hz: it then goes through exactly the board's chains, and `HR3_SAC` (for `R2`) runs on the
  bench's 50 Hz HR3 windows. Each record is one run of the probe applied; everything starts cold.
- **Reference beats.** Charlton's rules with our own detectors: two QRS detectors of different design
  (`hr1_bench.qrs_pan_tompkins`, band 5–15 Hz with integration; `hr1_bench.qrs_envelope`, band 8–30 Hz
  envelope peaks), a beat kept where both agree within 150 ms; a 20 s window dropped where they
  disagree or the PPG is flat for more than 0.2 s. Checked by eye before the run: the disagreements
  fall on ECG artefact (baseline jumps, clipping), the flats are the pleth clipped at 0 or 1. The
  first window (0–20 s) is not scored: start-up belongs to the dynamics criterion.
- **Beat scores.** Per record: the lag between the PPG beats and the ECG beats is searched from −10
  to +10 s in 20 ms steps and the one that detects the most is kept (the centre of that plateau, the
  one nearest zero); a reference beat is detected when the closest PPG beat is within 150 ms. Se =
  correct / reference, PPV = correct / detected, F1 their harmonic mean; median (IQR) over the
  records, as Charlton, and pooled.
- **Chains.** Read every 0.5 s. The ECG rate is 60 / mean of the last 5 reference RR ending at the
  output time minus the record's lag and the detector's latency, when those 6 beats lie in one run of
  kept windows. Wrong = more than 10 % off (`hr_bench.OK_REL`).
- **Differences from Charlton 2022, said before measuring.** Our detectors run causally and
  continuously over the record (his: offline, per 20 s window with 5 s overlap, zero-phase filters);
  one common front end (his: 0.67–8 Hz band-pass, then each detector's own steps); our QRS detectors
  (his: jqrs and rpeakdetect); the first 20 s not scored.

### Q3: chains against the ECG heart rate

| `HR1_TERMA` + `R2` | 74.5 | 1.8 | 0.836 | 64.7 | 74.5 | 8.3 |
| `HR1_SSF` + `R0` | 74.2 | 0.4 | 0.926 | 74.2 | 74.2 | **5.9** |
| `HR1_SSF` + `R1` | 95.7 | 7.6 | 0.911 | 68.8 | 84.0 | 14.2 |
| `HR1_SSF` + `R2` | 78.2 | 2.8 | 0.855 | 60.4 | 78.2 | 9.3 |
| `HR1_MSPTD` + `R0` (offline) | 85.5 | 1.1 | 0.967 | 85.4 | 85.5 | 4.8 |

| `HR1_MSPTD` + `R2` (offline) | 80.8 | 2.3 | 0.927 | 75.6 | 80.8 | 7.9 |

- **Today's rule is honest on neonates.** `R0` publishes 0.4–1.8 % wrong with every detector. What
  it costs is availability, and the **detector sets that cost**: with the same rule and about the same
  honesty, SSF publishes 74 % of the time, today's detector 63 %, TERMA 49 %. Rationale §5.4 had
  found that behind the gate the detector did not change the published values on the bench; on
  neonates it changes how often there is one.
- **TERMA + `R0` is wrong with confidence.** 173 of its 330 wrong values are half the rate, 147 of
  them with `hr1_sqi` ≥ 0.8 (post hoc count over the 100 records): missing every other beat
  regularly gives doubled RR that are as regular as the true ones, and the CV gate lets them through.
  No threshold on its index brings it to 1 % wrong. Today's detector does this much less (20 half-rate
  values) and SSF almost never (2).
- **`R1` publishes much more and is wrong much more often.** 87–96 % published, 3.4–11.9 % wrong.
  Post hoc, on 20 records of THR + `R1`: 275 of 345 wrong values came while its own quality (the
  accepted share of the last 8 RR) was below 0.75. It keeps publishing an old median while the
  detector is failing. Its index does tell (AUC 0.89–0.95): raised until at most 5 % is wrong, `R1`
  keeps 84–95 %; at 1 % it gives nothing over `R0` (THR 65.9 against 63.4 %, SSF 68.8 against 74.2 %).
- **`R2` sits in between**: 75–81 % published, 1.8–2.8 % wrong. It is the rule that rescues TERMA
  (1.8 % wrong at 74.5 % published, against 49.4 % with `R0`): `HR3_SAC`'s period rejects the
  doubled RR. It leans on family 3, so it is not an independent check.
- **Best live chain on this data: `HR1_SSF` + `R0`** (H + A 5.9, against 8.4 for today's chain);
  MSPTD is better still but decides up to 11 s late.
- **Sensitivity of the lag:** with Charlton's unrestricted ±10 s lag instead of the −1..+2 s window,
  the wrong share of every chain moves by 0.2 points or less.

## What it means (proposed, for Alex)

1. **The detector, not the rule, is the lever.** At the honesty today's rule already gives (≤ 1 %
   wrong), SSF publishes 11 points more often than today's detector on neonates; `R1` buys
   availability only by accepting more error. This revises the "neither a filter nor a detector
   addresses availability" of rationale §5.4, which was measured on adult and simulator signals.
2. **The papers' values do not fit neonates.** TERMA's 667 ms and SSF's 0.34 s eye-closing were set
   for adult rates and lose beats above 170 bpm. Part 2 would choose them, and today's detector's
   0.6 threshold, on the 100 training neonates and report on these 100 test ones.
3. **`R1` as defined should not ship.** Without a threshold on its own quality it publishes stale
   values while the detector fails. A threshold on it, or `R0`'s CV gate over its accepted RR, is a
   part-2 question.
4. **Nothing moves to the library from this part.** It is another instrument; F3 (our captures
   against the ward monitor's heart rate, and the MS100 for dynamics and range) must agree first.

### Dynamics and range

- **Start-up** (median, SUBJ08, 84 applications of the probe): today's detector on the common front
  end 2.5 s, TERMA 3.5 s, MSPTD 6.0 s, SSF 8.5 s (its 8 s learning period), the `R2` chains 12.5 s (they
  wait for `HR3_SAC`'s 10.24 s window), `HR3_SAC` 10.5 s, **the board's HR1 12.0 s**. The board's
  start-up is the library's DC estimator starting from zero: OT minus a DC estimate that is still
  rising stays far below zero, so no beat crosses the threshold until the estimate has caught up, some
  τ·ln(DC / AC) ≈ 1.6 s × ln(100) ≈ 7 s at 1 % modulation, plus the five RR.
- **MS100 steps:** every chain settles in a median of 0.25 s after the keyboard event; they do not
  separate the methods.
- **MS100 plateaus** (one session, 20–240 bpm; 20 bpm is outside the range for everybody): today's
  detector right on every plateau from 30 to 240 bpm, with `R0`; **SSF reads 60 at 30 bpm (it counts the
  dicrotic wave: its 0.34 s eye-closing is far shorter than a 2 s beat) and 120 at 240 bpm (the same
  0.34 s locks out every other beat)**; MSPTD right at 47.7 % of the 30 bpm plateau; TERMA right except
  part of 30 bpm.

## What F2 and F3 say together (proposed, for Alex)

1. **The front end is the cheapest gain.** Today's detector and rule behind the common front end,
   with nothing else changed: 47 → 66 % published at the same honesty, start-up 12 → 2.5 s, the same
   MS100 range; weighted total 26.1 → 14.5. It is the change the library rationale §5.5 left waiting
   for this report. **Done (Alex, 2026-11-16): lib v0.122**; the library reproduces the bench on the
   19 stored sessions (same validity in 99.97 % of 25 158 outputs, HR1 within 0.1 bpm in 99.77 %,
   `experiments/hr1_port_check_20261116.py`); lab v1.115 mirrors it.
2. **SSF is the better detector on neonates, with the papers' values it is not ready.** It publishes
   the most with `R0` on both instruments (74 % MIMIC, 82 % ours, honest on both), but its 0.34 s
   eye-closing fails at 240 bpm and lets the dicrotic wave in at 30 bpm, and its 8 s learning period
   delays the start. Part 2 would set those on the MIMIC training neonates.
3. **`R1` needs a gate before it can ship.** It wins the most availability, honestly on clean neonates,
   but is wrong 3–13 % under artefact and on MIMIC. A threshold on its own quality (AUC 0.74–0.95) or
   `R0`'s CV over its accepted beats: part 2.
4. **TERMA is out with the papers' values** (confident half-rate errors, a third of the beats lost
   above 170 bpm); `R2` only rescues it.
5. **The ECG trace in the photos needs the new protocol** before it can count beats.

### Why real slow pulses make false beats: the front end's high-pass (diagnostic, post hoc)

The false beats of SSF fall in the middle of the ECG interval (phase 0.3–0.7 of the RR, 89 % of them):
in the diastole, where the real pulse is flat. What they count is made by the common front end's
2nd-order high-pass at 0.5 Hz: on a single hump every 1.6 s it undershoots below the pulse's foot by
half the pulse height after the peak and climbs back during the diastole, and that climb is a second
upstroke. Same beats and metric as the lowrate study, through the front end:

| Front end high-pass | Secondary slope sum / main, median (p90) | Beats over 1/3 (qppg's threshold) | Undershoot below the foot, × pulse height |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (raw PPG, lowrate study) | 0.020 (0.083) | — | — |
| 0.5 Hz (v0.122) | 0.296 (0.364) | 21.1 % | 0.49 |
| 0.3 Hz | 0.163 (0.214) | 0.6 % | 0.23 |
| 0.2 Hz | 0.110 (0.161) | 0 % | 0.14 |

the high-pass corner (Se / PPV %, pooled):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 Hz (v0.122) | 98.8 / 98.5 | 94.4 / 87.7 | 99.3 / 77.7 | 98.4 / 79.0 |

- The library's rationale §5.5 took the 0.5 Hz from HR2 and argued that "at 30 bpm the corner leads the
  peak, but by the same amount on every beat, so the RR does not change". That holds for the peak, not

- The library as it is (THR 0.6) is the least affected: PPV 98.5 % at 0.5 Hz, 99.6 % at 0.3 Hz.

## Limits

- **Another instrument.** A bedside monitor's pleth, already filtered and auto-scaled, at 125 Hz,
  mostly from a hand or foot of babies in a NICU; our OT is raw, at 500 Hz, from our probe. The
  ranking of the detectors and the rules is what carries over, not the values.
- **Clean ECG only.** About a third of the windows survive the reference rules (Charlton kept about
  40 %, a median of 4.0 of 10 min). Windows where the ECG is disturbed, often by the same movement
  that disturbs the PPG, are not scored: the numbers are optimistic for artefact.
- **No age.** MIMIC PERform does not say which babies are preterm; the clinical database that would
  needs Alex's PhysioNet credential.
- **One heart-rate reference for chains.** The mean of 5 ECG RR at the matched time; chains with a
  median (`R1`, `R2`) and the mean of `R0` read the same beats differently, and fast changes of rate
  count against all of them.
- **Two corrections after the first run**, before the chains' results were read as findings (both in
  the script's docstring): the beat-count lag aliases by whole beats on a regular rhythm (lags from −9
  to +9 s across records), so Q3 searches it within −1..+2 s, and the sensitivity check above shows it
  barely matters; MSPTD's beats arrive 0–11 s late, so the ECG rate is read over the beats a chain's
  value was computed from (this one mattered: MSPTD + `R0` went from 3.9 to 1.1 % wrong). An attempt
  to align by the pattern of the rate gave spurious lags and was dropped.
- **Post hoc** (marked as such above): the classes of TERMA's errors and the cause of `R1`'s; the
  corrected range check of part 2 (after the first run, on Alex's review of the MS100's 30 bpm pulse)
  and its high-pass diagnostic.
- **The real slow pulses are adults'** (PhysioNet/CinC 2015, ICU): no open neonatal PPG below 87 bpm
  was found, so how a neonate's slow pulse comes out of the high-pass is not measured.
- **The test neonates have now been read by three runs** (part 2, its correction, part 3). The
  procedure changed between runs because of the range check (Alex's review of the MS100, then the
  slow-pulse diagnostic), never because of a test result, and each choice was made on training only;
