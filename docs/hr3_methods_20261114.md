# The methods of family 3 (HR3) against the ward monitor, measured (2026-11-14)

Script: `experiments/hr3_methods_20261114.py`. Bench: `pulsenest/hr_bench.py` (new, with its test
`test_pulsenest/hr_bench_test.py`). Data: the 17 HOSPNAV sessions of SUBJ08–11 with a reference and
the two MS100 sessions, 23 940 HR3 windows, the same as on 2026-10-21 and 2026-11-12. Outputs
(tables and one figure per session) in `captures/sessions/HR3_METHODS_20261114/`, not versioned.
Offline only: no board, no new capture. Nothing in the library was changed and nothing is adopted.

Method labels (`docs/nomenclature.md`): `HR3_HPS` = today's HR3 (harmonic product spectrum),
`HR3_SAC` = autocorrelation of the spectrum along frequency with local contrast (2026-11-12),
`HR3_CEP` = cepstrum.

## Why

Phase 1 of the work plan drafted on 2026-11-13: which methods form family 3, and by what rule. The
two earlier studies scored the pulse rate against the consensus of the board's HR1 and HR2, which
exists only on clean stretches: 37 / 56 / 0 / 38 % of the windows of SUBJ08 / 09 / 10 / 11. On
those windows `HR3_SAC` already read neither double nor half, so a combination had nothing to fix
there. What mattered was outside, where there was no reference.

## Questions

Agreed with Alex on 2026-11-14 before measuring.

1. Outside the clean windows, on which octave is each method?
2. Where does today's HR3 publish one third of the rate, and what do the other methods say there?
3. Is the cepstrum right, and does it fail where `HR3_SAC` fails or elsewhere?
4. Does a combination fix anything `HR3_SAC` alone does not?
5. What rule would form `hr3` and `hr3_sqi`, and what does it do to the SpO2 gate?
6. Does any method of family 3 cover the simulator's 30 bpm?

## A reference that exists during artefacts

The ward monitor (Mindray BeneVision N17) was photographed every 10 s by the VideoNest phone, and
the 1453 snapshots were read by eye on 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17
(`captures/sessions/BIAS_ANALYSIS_SUBJ10_SUBJ11/ocr_vs_eye/`). The number at the top right of the
screen is the heart rate from the ECG for SUBJ08, SUBJ09 and SUBJ11. SUBJ10 had no ECG trace on
screen and that number equals the monitor's own pulse rate in every snapshot, so for SUBJ10 the
reference is another pulse oximeter.

- **Pairing.** A window takes the reading nearest to its end, at most 6 s away; the monitor's
  number and the 10.24 s window both look back about 10 s. The phone clock is corrected per session
  with the recorder's `offset_ms` (under 40 ms for SUBJ08, 0.4 s for SUBJ10 and SUBJ11, 1.95 s for
  SUBJ09).
- **Stable readings only.** A reading is scored when the readings before and after it are within
  10 % of it, so a rate in transition is not counted as an error.
- **Correct** means within 10 % of the reference, as in the earlier studies. Errors are classed by
  the harmonic ratio they sit on (within 8 %): double, half, triple, third, 3/2, 2/3, or *other*.
- **Clean / not clean.** A window is *clean* when the consensus of HR1 and HR2 exists.

## Methods

Fixed in the script's docstring before the first run. Front end: the library's HR3 chain (512
samples at 50 Hz, Hann, one window every 0.5 s).

| Label | What it is |
|---|---|
| `HR3_HPS` | today's HR3: product of three harmonics, published while its SQI is above 0 |
| B1 (HPS2) | the two-harmonic product of 2026-10-21 |
| `HR3_SAC` | spectral autocorrelation of the local contrast, first maximum reaching 0.8 of the highest |
| `HR3_CEP` cep40, cep60 | cepstrum of the band 0.49–15 Hz of the same spectrum, in dB above a floor 40 or 60 dB under the band maximum |
| `HR3_CEP` cepc6 | cepstrum of the local contrast that `HR3_SAC` reads |
| R2 | `HR3_SAC`, published only if the cepstrum agrees within 10 % |
| R3 | `HR3_SAC`, unless the two differ by a factor 2 or 3 and only the cepstrum agrees with the board's HR2 |
| board HR1, board HR2 | what the firmware published at the window's last sample |

The cepstrum here is the magnitude of the Fourier transform, along frequency, of the band's
sequence (mean removed, Hann taper, zero-padded to 2048: quefrency step 5 ms). The peak is searched
between the periods of 263 and 27 bpm and the smallest quefrency whose maximum reaches 0.8 of the
highest is taken. No threshold was tuned.

**Post hoc**, added after the first run and not tuned: `HR3_SAC` *gated* (published only while
rho ≥ 0.5 and comb purity ≥ 0.3, the validity rule of 2026-11-12 applied to the rate), and the
table of what each other method gets right where `HR3_SAC` does not.

## Results

**A. The bench reproduces the earlier numbers, and the two references agree.** Every row of the
2026-11-12 tables comes out identical (rate: 15 rows, largest difference 0.0 points; SpO2 gate:
0.0 points published, 0.00 Arms). Where a window has both references, the consensus is within 10 %
of the monitor in 100.0 % of them (table above). That checks at once the by-eye transcription, the
clock alignment and the consensus reference of the earlier studies.

*Hypothesis, not measured:* at 166–175 bpm one third of the heart rate is 55–58 per minute, which
is a neonatal breathing rate (the three snapshots opened on 2026-11-14 show 62, 42 and 66 breaths
per minute). A respiratory modulation of the PPG at f0/3 with its second harmonic would give the
three-harmonic product a candidate at f0/3 whose "third harmonic" is the cardiac fundamental. The
respiration rate is on the snapshots but was not transcribed.

right in about half of the few windows where `HR3_SAC` is not.** Share of the windows with a stable
reading:

| | SUBJ08 | SUBJ09 | SUBJ11 | SUBJ10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Both right (cep40) | 92.8 | 90.7 | 99.0 | 37.6 |
| Only `HR3_SAC` right | 5.1 | 7.8 | 0.7 | 14.2 |
| Only the cepstrum right | 0.9 | 0.9 | 0.1 | 10.0 |
| Neither | 1.2 | 0.6 | 0.2 | 38.3 |

The preparation matters more than the method: with a 60 dB floor the cepstrum reads half the rate
in 7.9 / 24.4 / 12.1 % of the windows; with 40 dB, in 1.2 / 3.5 / 0.3 %; on the local contrast, in
0.5 / 4.1 / 1.9 %. The local contrast is the same sequence `HR3_SAC` reads, and its cepstrum is the
Fourier pair of that autocorrelation: same information, and the lag axis picks better than the
quefrency axis.

*Post hoc.* What each other method gets right where `HR3_SAC` does not (SUBJ08 / 09 / 11, % of the
windows): cepstrum cep40 0.9 / 0.9 / 0.1; cepc6 0.7 / 0.3 / 0.1; board HR2 0.2 / 0.3 / 0.0; board

and any of them can gain a point.

- R3 (octave vote with HR2) changes nothing or loses a little (SUBJ11: 99.7 against 99.8 % with

- R2 (publish only when the cepstrum agrees, cep40) removes most wrong values at a cost in

**G. (Question 6) No method measured here covers 30 bpm except the ones in use today.** MS100, share
right per plateau: `HR3_HPS`, B1 and board HR1 100 % at 30 bpm; board HR2 25.2 %; `HR3_SAC` 0 %;
the cepstrum 1.3 / 40.4 / 6.0 % (cep40 / cep60 / cepc6). Every method reads 100 % from 60 to
240 bpm. The simulator has no plateau between 30 and 60 bpm, so where `HR3_SAC` starts to work is
not known.

**H. By-product: the board's HR1 and HR2 against the ECG.** HR1 publishes little (42 / 59 / 43 % of
the windows; a quarter to a third outside the clean ones) and is right in 99.5–99.9 % of what it
publishes. HR2 publishes more (78 / 84 / 98 %) and is right in 97.6 / 94.2 / 96.2 % of it; outside
the clean windows of SUBJ09 one value in seven is wrong. Both were until now judged only against a
reference they are part of.

## Limits

- One reading every 10 s, of a number the monitor itself averages over several seconds; windows
  where the readings change by more than 10 % are left out (0–6 % of the windows), so fast changes
  of rate are not judged. 17 % of SUBJ08's windows have no snapshot within 6 s.
- SUBJ10's reference is another pulse oximeter under the same motion; in SUBJ11 the monitor's own
  pulse rate is more than 10 % away from the ECG in 10 of 235 snapshots. Whether SUBJ10 had ECG
  electrodes is to be confirmed by Alex.
- Three subjects with ECG, all between 122 and 195 bpm, all resting or handled in a cot. Nothing
  below 120 bpm except the simulator, which has no plateau between 30 and 60 bpm.
- The cepstrum was run in three preparations fixed beforehand, with one picker; a cepstrum tuned
  for this signal could do better. The result says these three do not beat `HR3_SAC`.
- IR channel only. Parameters of `HR3_SAC` are those fixed on 2026-11-12 after seeing SUBJ10; no
  cross-validation.
- The respiration hypothesis of C is not tested.
- No firmware cost was measured for any method.

## Decisions for Alex

1. **The rate of family 3.** `HR3_SAC` is right in 98–100 % of the windows against the ECG, where
   today's HR3 is right in 64–83 % and wrong in 4–6 %. Proposed: take `HR3_SAC` forward as the
   candidate to replace `HR3_HPS` as the rate of `hr3`, and measure its cost on the board.
2. **30 to 60 bpm.** `HR3_SAC` fails at 30 bpm and today's HR3 does not. Before any replacement:
   a bench capture on the MS100 at 35, 40, 45, 50 and 55 bpm to find where `HR3_SAC` starts to
   work, and then a rule for the low rates (keep `HR3_HPS` there, or a longer window).
3. **The cepstrum.** It does not earn a place as a rate method, and as a validity check the gate on
   rho and comb purity does better under motion. Proposed: drop `HR3_CEP` from the plan.
4. **Validity of `hr3`.** rho ≥ 0.5 with purity ≥ 0.3 gives a rate that is wrong in 0.0–0.1 % of the
   windows (0.5 % in SUBJ10) and an SpO2 gate equal to today's. Thresholds are those of 2026-11-12, not tuned:
   proposed for phase 4 (across families), with the joint SQI analysis.
5. **Today's HR3, meanwhile.** The one-third readings are in the field now. A stop-gap inside the
   library (for instance a floor on `hr3_sqi`, whose median is 0.45–0.80 there against 1.00) could
   be measured on this bench before `HR3_SAC` is ready.
6. **The respiration hypothesis.** Reading the respiration rate off the snapshots of the one-third
   episodes would test it; a few dozen snapshots.
