# The methods of family 2 (HR2) against the ward monitor, measured (2026-11-15)

Script: `experiments/hr2_methods_20261115.py`. Bench: `pulsenest/hr_bench.py` (time-lag family
added the same day: `hr2_segments`, `lag_sweep`, `hr2_acf`, `hr2_mpm`, `hr2_yin`; checks in
`test_pulsenest/hr_bench_test.py`). Data: the 17 HOSPNAV sessions of SUBJ08–11 and the two MS100
sessions (2026-10-19 and 2026-10-21), 24 160 HR2 windows. Outputs (tables, one figure per ward session) in
`captures/sessions/HR2_METHODS_20261115/`, not versioned. Offline only: no board, no new capture,
nothing in the library changed, nothing adopted. Parts 2 and 3 (the same day, at the end): MPM and
YIN with parameters chosen out of sample, and whether `HR2_ACF` should be parametrised better. Part 1's
YIN summed its difference over N − τ samples; part 2 shows that this changed little.

Method labels (`docs/nomenclature.md`): `HR2_ACF` = today's HR2 (unbiased normalised
autocorrelation), `HR2_MPM` = McLeod pitch method (normalised square difference function, NSDF),
`HR2_YIN` = YIN (cumulative mean normalised difference, CMND).

## Why

Plan of 2026-11-15 for HR2LAB: before a live window shows the methods of family 2 side by side, the
bench says how they score. MPM and YIN were planned on 2026-09-02 because pitch detection designed
them against the octave error of the autocorrelation. The comparison uses the weighted criteria
agreed with Alex the same day: honesty when failing 35, availability 20, dynamics 15, range and low
perfusion 15, precision when right 10, cost 5, plus a map of where each method fails.

## Questions

Agreed with Alex on 2026-11-15 before measuring.

1. Do MPM or YIN fix the octave errors of `HR2_ACF`?
2. Honesty: what share of the published windows is wrong, and does the quality index tell?
3. Availability at the same published error.
4. Dynamics: start-up from the probe applied, and the steps of the MS100.
5. Range: the MS100 plateaus, 30 bpm above all.
6. Map of gaps: where does the best lag method fail, and does it fail where `HR3_SAC` fails?

## Methods

Fixed in the script's docstring before the first run.

- **Front end.** The library's HR2 chain: band-pass 0.5–5 Hz at the AFE rate, decimated to 50 Hz,
  400 samples (8 s), a window every 0.5 s, runs of the probe applied processed apart.
- **One sweep.** For lags 0 to 112: `r(τ) = Σ x[i]·x[i+τ]` and `m(τ) = Σ (x[i]² + x[i+τ]²)`. The three
  methods read it differently:

  ```
  HR2_ACF   A(τ)  = r(τ)·N / (r(0)·(N − τ))      first local maximum ≥ 0.5           quality = A at the peak
  HR2_MPM   n(τ)  = 2·r(τ) / m(τ)                first key maximum ≥ k·highest, k=0.9  quality = clarity, published ≥ 0.5
  HR2_YIN   d'(τ) = d(τ)·τ / Σ d(1..τ),  d = m − 2r   first dip under 0.10, down to its minimum   quality = 1 − d'
  ```

  Search between 263 and 27 bpm, parabolic interpolation, published between 30 and 260 bpm. The k
  and the threshold are the papers' values, not tuned.
- **Reference.** The monitor reading nearest to the window's end (≤ 6 s), scored only where stable;
  correct = within 10 %. ECG heart rate for SUBJ08, SUBJ09, SUBJ11 (pooled as "ECG"); another
  pulse oximeter for SUBJ10, reported apart. The consensus of HR1 and HR2 is not used: it contains
  the board's HR2.
- **Rows for comparison.** `HR3_SAC`, `HR3_SAC` gated (rho ≥ 0.5 and comb purity ≥ 0.3),
  `HR3_HPS` on the HR3 window ending at the same sample; board HR1 and HR2.
- **Weighted total.** Each criterion on a fixed scale, set before computing and not taken from
  the data. A method gets the criterion's whole weight at the bad end of the scale and nothing at
  the good end, in proportion in between. 0 = best on all, 100 = worst on all.

  | Criterion (weight) | Metric | Scores 0 at | Full weight at |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Honesty (35) | wrong of published | 0 % | 20 % |
  | Availability (20) | published, at the same operating point as honesty | 100 % | 0 % |
  | Dynamics (15) | start-up median + MS100 step median | 0 s | 20 s |
  | Range (15) | MS100 right, mean over the plateaus | 100 % | 0 % |
  | Precision (10) | median absolute error when right | 0 bpm | 5 bpm |
  | Cost (5) | not measured offline | 0 for all | — |

  It replaces a ranking by places (place 1–3 times the weight), at Alex's request on 2026-11-15.
  Places count order, not size, so a gap of 0.7 points took a whole weight. A scale running
  between the best and worst of three methods would do the same with a tiny spread, so the scales
  are fixed. The replaced version also measured availability at 1 % wrong, a different operating
  point from honesty, against the criteria agreed that morning. That figure is now shown and not
  scored.

**Post hoc**, added after the first run showed the papers' pickers jumping to multiples of the
period: MPM with k = 0.8, 0.7, 0.5; YIN with threshold 0.2, 0.3, 0.5; the NSDF read with
`HR2_ACF`'s picker. They separate the function from the picker. Nothing was adopted from them.

**The bench mirrors the board.** The bench's `HR2_ACF` against the HR2 the board published, per
session: same validity in 96–100 % of the windows, the same class (within 10 %) in 99.5–100 %
(SUBJ10: 89–100 %, motion). Within 1 bpm in 82–99.8 %; the differences are under ±3 %, never an
octave, and grow where the recording drops samples. The board's HR2 row and the bench's `HR2_ACF`
row agree in every table below to within 0.4 points.

**Answer to Q1: no, they make it worse.** On real neonatal PPG the first peak of the lag function,
the one at the beat, is often lower than the peaks two or more beats later. In one SUBJ11 window
at 164 bpm the autocorrelation reads 0.75 at one period, 0.85 at two and 0.88 at five. The
variability from beat to beat and the respiratory modulation make the signal resemble itself
better a whole breath later. Speech, for which MPM and YIN were designed, has the tallest peak
first. With the papers' values MPM jumps to two periods and YIN to up to five. `HR2_ACF`'s
absolute bar (the first peak above 0.5) is what fits this signal.

**Answer to Q2:** `HR2_ACF` publishes a wrong value in 4.4 % of its windows, against 17.5 % for
MPM and YIN. Its quality index separates right from wrong best (AUC 0.85); in SUBJ11 none does
(0.34–0.60).

## Limits

- Three subjects with ECG, all between 122 and 195 bpm, resting or handled in a cot. Nothing below
  120 bpm except the simulator, which has no plateau between 30 and 60 bpm.
- The respiratory explanation of the tall later peaks rests on what the curves show; the
  respiration rate was not measured.
- One reading every 10 s; windows where the readings change by more than 10 % are left out.
- The bench and the board differ by up to ±3 % in a few percent of the windows, probably samples the
  recording dropped; no class changes.
- The post hoc values were chosen after seeing the data, on the same data. No cross-validation.
- No firmware cost was measured. On the board MPM and YIN would cost one prefix sum and one pass
  over the lags on top of today's sweep.
- IR channel only.

## Part 2: MPM and YIN with parameters chosen on this signal

Script: `experiments/hr2_tuning_20261115.py` (same day, after the first part). Outputs in
`captures/sessions/HR2_TUNING_20261115/`, not versioned.

**Why.** Alex: the first part leaves MPM and YIN in an unfair position. They are close relatives of
`HR2_ACF`, so their result probably comes from the papers' values, which were set for speech.
Reviewing the bench before this run found a second possible cause in YIN: its difference was summed
over N − τ samples, so it shrank as the lag grew and favoured long lags. The paper (eq. 6) uses a
fixed integration window. The bench now does too (`hr_bench.yin_difference`, the newest N − 112
samples against their copies τ earlier). `HR2LAB` shows YIN with the fixed window, and the first
part's script keeps the shrinking one so its tables stay reproducible.

**Fixed before this run** (docstring of the script):
- The choice maximises one score: ok % − 1.75 × wrong %, both as a share of the windows with a
  stable reference. 1.75 = honesty 35 / availability 20.
- Leave one subject out over the three ECG subjects. The parameters are chosen on two subjects and
  scored on the third, three times, and the held-out windows are pooled. SUBJ10 and the MS100 never
  take part in a choice.
- Grids: `HR2_MPM` k 0.50–0.95 × clarity to publish 0.3–0.7; `HR2_YIN` threshold 0.05–0.60;
  `HR2_ACF` min_corr 0.30–0.80. The front end is the library's.

### T1: YIN's window

| `HR2_YIN`, threshold 0.10 | Published | Right | Wrong of published | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shrinking window (part 1) | 35.8 % | 29.5 % | 17.7 % | 18.4 |
| fixed window (the paper) | 37.9 % | 31.7 % | 16.4 % | 20.9 |

The window was a real defect, but a small one. What kept YIN down was its threshold.

every method is to take the first peak or the first dip that clears a bar. That is `HR2_ACF`'s
rule. The differences that remain are in the curve's normalisation and, above all, in the quality
index.

## Part 3: should HR2_ACF be parametrised better?

Same script, same score, same leave-one-subject-out procedure. min_corr is chosen inside each front
end. The rule for proposing a change to the library was fixed before the run:
- the pooled score rises by at least 2 points, no held-out subject loses more than 1 point, and the
  MS100 plateaus at or under 60 bpm are not worse;
- or start-up falls by at least 1 s for at most 1 point of score.

### A1: hr2_min_corr

All three folds choose 0.4 or 0.45. Out of sample the score goes from 75.1 to 76.4. That is +1.3,
under the 2 points of the rule.
- Per subject: SUBJ08 −0.6, SUBJ09 +1.0, SUBJ11 +4.5.
- SUBJ10 loses 2.8 points: it publishes more, and more of it wrong.

**Not proposed.** A lower bar helps where the signal is good and hurts where it is not.

### A2: the front end

min_corr is chosen out of sample within each front end:

| High edge | Buffer | Score | Wrong of published | Start-up, median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Hz | 4 / 6 / 8 s | 66.7 / 68.2 / 66.9 | 8.4–9.5 % | 4 / 6 / 8 s |
| 4 Hz | 4 / 6 / 8 s | 72.4 / 72.5 / 73.5 | 5.6–7.4 % | 4 / 6 / 8 s |
| 5 Hz (library) | 4 / 6 / 8 s | 74.5 / 76.1 / 76.4 | 4.7–6.5 % | 4 / 6 / 8 s |

A lower high edge makes it worse at every buffer length. The second harmonic helps the
autocorrelation find the period, so the library's 5 Hz stays.

The buffer is the real lever. **Post hoc P3** checks the 5 Hz front ends where no choice was made,
with the library's min_corr of 0.5:

| Buffer | Score, ECG | SUBJ08 / 09 / 11 | Wrong of published | Start-up, median / 90th percentile | SUBJ10 | MS100 30 bpm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 s (library) | 75.1 | 72.1 / 70.2 / 88.2 | 4.4 % | 8.0 / 15.6 s | 0.0 | 50.3 % |
| 6 s | 74.9 | 71.1 / 70.4 / 88.2 | 4.8 % | 6.0 / 10.6 s | −2.1 | 50.3 % |
| 4 s | 73.9 | 70.9 / 69.2 / 86.6 | 5.7 % | 4.0 / 6.2 s | −11.3 | 25.2 % |

### A3: the rule

- **6 s at min_corr 0.5 passes the start-up branch.** Start-up is 2 s faster at the median and 5 s
  faster at the 90th percentile. That costs 0.2 points of score and 0.4 points of wrong values.
  - Nothing changes at 30 bpm or on the plateaus at or under 60 bpm.
  - SUBJ08 loses 1.0 point and SUBJ10 loses 2.1.
  - The sweep also gets cheaper, with 300 samples instead of 400.
- **4 s fails.** It loses 1.2 points, halves the 30 bpm plateau, because the buffer no longer holds
  the period, and does much worse on SUBJ10.
- **min_corr fails.** See A1.

## Limits of parts 2 and 3

- Three ECG subjects, so three folds. A choice made on two neonates and checked on a third is a
  small sample. The folds agree, which is encouraging but is not proof.
- The score's 1.75 comes from the agreed weights. Another penalty would move the choices, and the
  answer to T2 depends on it more than the ordering of A2 does.
- P1, P2 and P3 were added after the first run of the script. P3 only checks candidates that the
  rule fixed before the run let through.
- Start-up is measured on the bench's windows. Its median equals the buffer length because the
  first window is usually right. The 90th percentile is where the buffers differ.
- SUBJ10 is hard for the whole family and its reference is another pulse oximeter. Losses there
  are a warning, not a measurement of the ECG kind.
- Cost was not measured. A 6 s buffer is cheaper, and MPM and YIN cost about the same as
  `HR2_ACF`, but none of that was measured on a board.

## Decisions for Alex

These replace the first part's decisions.

1. **MPM and YIN.**
   - With the papers' values they stay out.
   - With parameters chosen for PPG they become about as honest as `HR2_ACF`, but publish fewer
     right values: the score keeps `HR2_ACF` first by 6–10 points.
   - On the weighted total, tuned MPM ties `HR2_ACF`: 22.3 against 22.2.
   - Proposed: do not port either as a replacement for `HR2_ACF`. A tie does not pay for a change
     in the library.
2. **YIN's quality index.**
   - The one thing family 2 learns here: YIN's 1 − d' tells right from wrong better than
     `HR2_ACF`'s peak height. Out of sample, at 1 % wrong, it keeps 70.6 % of the windows against
     50.2 %.
   - Proposed: carry it into the phase that combines methods, as a candidate quality index for
     `HR2_ACF`'s lag, or as a cross-check. Not a change to the library now.
3. **The low rates.** Tuned MPM and YIN read 30 bpm where `HR2_ACF` reads it half the time. Proposed:
   weigh it with the pending MS100 capture at 35–55 bpm, in the phase across families.
4. **`HR2_ACF` min_corr and band.** Keep 0.5 and 0.5–5 Hz.
5. **`HR2_ACF` buffer of 6 s instead of 8 s.**
   - The one change the rule lets through: start-up 2 s faster at the median and 5 s at the 90th
